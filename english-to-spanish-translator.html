How to get the best English to Spanish translations

Write in complete sentences with correct punctuation — the translator understands context far better from a full sentence than from single words. Keep slang and very local idioms to a minimum, and if a result looks odd, try rephrasing the English more simply and translate again.

Spanish has formal and informal ways of saying “you” (usted vs tú). Machine translation usually picks the neutral option; for business or official writing, review the result or ask a native speaker. Verb endings also change with the subject, so include the subject in your sentence (“She works…” rather than just “Works…”) for a more accurate result.
